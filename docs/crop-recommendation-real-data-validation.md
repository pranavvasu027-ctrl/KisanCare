## 2. Task 1: Investigation of Rainfall Feature Semantics & Scale

A primary objective of this audit was to resolve the discrepancy between real-world rainfall inputs in India and the scale expected by the underlying model.

### 2.1 Audit of Model 1 Training Distribution
The training data for Model 1 originated from the benchmark Kaggle Crop Recommendation dataset authored by Atharva Ingle (`data/soil_validation/Crop_recommendation_training_reference.csv`, 2,200 samples across 22 crops):

```
Feature: rainfall (mm)
  Count: 2200
  Mean:  103.5 mm
  Std:    54.9 mm
  Min:    20.2 mm
  25%:    64.9 mm
  50%:    94.9 mm
  75%:   124.3 mm
  Max:   298.6 mm
```

### 2.2 Decision Tree Threshold Extraction
Auditing the internal decision trees of `model1_npk.pkl` (`RandomForestClassifier`, 100 estimators) revealed the exact internal split thresholds applied to the `rainfall` feature:
- The lowest rainfall split threshold is **$20.84\text{ mm}$** (separating Muskmelon and drought-tolerant pulses).
- The intermediate split thresholds center around **$100–180\text{ mm}$** (separating upland staples like Maize, Bajra, and Cotton).
- The maximum internal split threshold across all 100 estimators is **$264.03\text{ mm}$**.

### 2.3 Why Dividing Annual Rainfall by 12 is Agronomically Invalid
While Sheshank2609's model card casually referenced "20–3000 mm/year", the authors trained the Random Forest directly on Atharva Ingle's raw unscaled features without any preprocessing transform. We evaluated whether dividing cumulative annual or seasonal rainfall by 12 (to approximate "monthly rainfall") was defensible:
1. **Monsoon Asymmetry:** In Maharashtra, 75–85% of annual precipitation falls within the 100–120 days of the Southwest Monsoon (Kharif season). Rainfall does not distribute evenly across 12 months.
2. **Short-Duration & Zaid Crops:** Dividing 800 mm by 12 yields $66.7\text{ mm}$. In Model 1, a rainfall input of $66.7\text{ mm}$ routes decision trees toward low-moisture summer/zaid crops (like Muskmelon, Watermelon, and Chickpea), severely penalizing Kharif staples like Soybean, Cotton, and Paddy that require abundant moisture during active vegetative and tillering phases.
3. **Absence of Provenance:** Atharva Ingle's original dataset documentation never specified a temporal duration; it simply stated "rainfall in mm". Dividing by 12 represents an unverified mathematical assumption that distorts crop classification.

### 2.4 Adopted Engineering & Agronomic Solution
1. **Preserve Input Contract:** Retain the model's documented input range ($20.0–3000.0\text{ mm}$).
2. **Explicit Scientific Extrapolation Warning:** Whenever the user enters rainfall exceeding $298.6\text{ mm}$ (Model 1's training maximum), the pipeline emits an explicit warning:
   > *"Rainfall ({rainfall:.1f} mm) exceeds Model 1 training maximum (298.6 mm). Model 1 was trained on raw monthly/critical-stage rainfall (20.2–298.6 mm) rather than cumulative annual precipitation. Values > 264 mm saturate Random Forest tree splits, routing to the wet-climate branch favoring Rice/Jute. Evaluate recommendations in conjunction with regional historical presence."*
3. **No Retraining Without Valid Data:** As mandated, no unverified model retraining was attempted.

## 3. Task 2: Regional Evidence Safeguard & False Positive Resolution

### 3.1 The Coffee in Sangli Problem (Root Cause Analysis)
In Phase 3 testing of `REC_06` (Miraj block, Sangli district; Kharif season; $N=105, P=30, K=55, \text{pH}=7.0, T=35^\circ\text{C}, H=68\%, \text{Rainfall}=900\text{ mm}$), the initial integration produced:
- **Rank #1: Coffee (Combined Score: 0.3930)**
- **Rank #2: Rice (Combined Score: 0.3166)**
- **Rank #3: Jowar (Combined Score: 0.3052)**

**Agronomic Impossibility:** Sangli is a semi-arid, hot Deccan plateau district ($550\text{ m}$ elevation, black Vertisol clay). Coffee (*Coffea arabica / canephora*) is an acid-loving ($\text{pH } 5.5–6.5$), high-elevation ($800–1500\text{ m}$), shade-tolerant hill plantation crop cultivated almost exclusively in Karnataka's Western Ghats (Kodagu, Chikmagalur, Hassan) and Kerala. Zero commercial coffee is grown in Sangli.

**Mathematical Trace:**
The multi-criteria engine blends Model 1 ($w_1 = 0.60$) and Model 2 ($w_2 = 0.40$):
$$\text{Combined Score} = w_1 \cdot s_1 + w_2 \cdot s_2$$
- In Model 1: At $T = 35^\circ\text{C}$ and $N=105$, the Random Forest assigned Coffee a high class probability: $s_1 = 0.6550$.
- In Model 2: The 26-year Maharashtra APY census table for Sangli Kharif contains zero records for Coffee: $s_2 = 0.0$.
- Composite: $0.60 \times 0.6550 + 0.40 \times 0.0 = 0.3930$.
- Regionally proven crops like Rice ($s_1 = 0.200, s_2 = 0.4916 \implies 0.3166$) and Jowar ($s_1 = 0.005, s_2 = 0.7554 \implies 0.3052$) were outscored by Coffee simply because Model 1 carried 60% weight and Model 2 did not penalize unverified crops.

### 3.2 The Evidence-Aware Safeguard Design
Rather than hardcoding an arbitrary crop-specific rule (`if crop == 'coffee'`), we implemented a general, data-driven safeguard rooted in the 26-year Maharashtra APY agricultural census:

1. **Regional Support Verification:**
   For every candidate crop evaluated by the pipeline:
   $$\text{has\_regional} = (m_2 \text{ entry is not None and } s_2 > 0.0)$$
   - Crops present in the district/season census with positive suitability ($s_2 > 0$) are classified as `is_regionally_supported = True`.
   - Crops with 0 historical records in that district and season are classified as `is_regionally_supported = False`.

2. **Categorical Evidence Tiering:**
   - **Tier 1 (Regionally Verified):** `evidence_tier = f"Verified in {district}"`
   - **Tier 2 (Biophysical Candidate):** `evidence_tier = f"Biophysical Candidate Only (Unverified in {district})"`

3. **Regional Uncertainty Discount Factor:**
   When a crop lacks regional cultivation records, recommending it involves substantial agronomic and commercial risk. A 50% uncertainty factor is applied to its composite score:
   $$\text{Score}_{\text{final}} = \begin{cases} 
   w_1 s_1 + w_2 s_2 & \text{if } \text{has\_regional} = \text{True} \\
   (w_1 s_1 + w_2 s_2) \times 0.50 & \text{if } \text{has\_regional} = \text{False}
   \end{cases}$$
   For Coffee in Sangli:
   $$\text{Score}_{\text{Coffee}} = (0.60 \times 0.6550) \times 0.50 = 0.1965$$

4. **Candidate Prioritization & Ranking:**
   Candidates are sorted hierarchically:
   $$\text{sort\_key} = (\text{is\_regionally\_supported}, \text{combined\_score}, \text{npk\_score})$$
   This guarantees that:
   - Regionally supported crops always take precedence over unverified biophysical candidates.
   - The top recommendation (`prediction`) presented to the farmer is guaranteed to be a regionally verified crop whenever regional census data exists.
   - Biophysical candidates are never silently deleted; they remain accessible to researchers and agronomists under their explicit unverified tier.

5. **Transparent User Interface Controls:**
   - Added a dual-view filter toggle in `CropRecommendation.tsx`: **"All Candidates"** vs **"Regionally Verified Only"**.
   - Added visual badges distinguishing *Verified in {district}* (emerald) from *Biophysical Candidate Only* (amber with alert icon).
   - Displayed *0 Records (Unverified)* in the Census History cell for unverified crops.

## 6. Guidance for Interpreting Model Recommendations vs Regional Evidence

Agronomists and farm advisors should interpret KisanCare's dual-score outputs using the following framework:

### 1. Model 1 (NPK Random Forest Score)
- **What it represents:** Theoretical biophysical and physiological compatibility.
- **Interpretation:** Indicates whether the crop can survive and complete its vegetative cycle given laboratory soil nutrient levels ($N, P, K, \text{pH}$) and ambient climate ($T, H, R$).
- **Caveat:** High biophysical probability does **not** indicate that the crop is commercially viable, pest-adapted, or historically successful in the farmer's specific agro-ecological zone.

### 2. Model 2 (Regional Viability Score)
- **What it represents:** 26-year empirical agricultural history from Maharashtra APY census records (1997–2022).
- **Interpretation:** Reflects actual acreage, frequency of cultivation across decades, and average yields achieved by real farmers in that district and season.
- **Caveat:** Does not evaluate real-time soil test chemistry; solely evaluates historical geographic patterns.

### 3. The Combined Blended Score
- **What it represents:** Evidence-weighted multi-criteria recommendation ($60\% \text{ Biophysical} + 40\% \text{ Regional}$).
- **Interpretation:** Balances laboratory soil suitability with regional cultivation proof.

### 4. Interpreting Evidence Tiers:
- **"Verified in {district}":** Proven regional track record with established seed availability, local market mandis, and institutional extension support. High confidence for standard farm planning.
- **"Biophysical Candidate Only (Unverified in {district})":** Scientifically possible under soil/climate parameters, but carries **zero historical commercial evidence** in the district. Should only be considered for small-scale experimental trials, university research plots, or protected cultivation with confirmed market off-takers.

## 7. Remaining Architectural Limitations & Future Recommendations

1. **Model 1 Rainfall Saturation:**
   Model 1 cannot distinguish between $400\text{ mm}$, $800\text{ mm}$, and $2000\text{ mm}$ of cumulative rainfall. When seasonal totals are entered, Model 1 consistently routes to wet-climate leaves, biasing predictions toward Rice. In a future model training cycle, the biophysical classifier should be retrained on explicit cumulative growing-season rainfall or paired with an evapotranspiration water-balance model.
2. **Horticultural & Perennial Crops:**
   Certain high-value perennial crops (e.g. Grapes in Nashik, Oranges in Nagpur, Pomegranates in Solapur) are cultivated over multi-year cycles and are under-represented in single-season benchmark datasets.
3. **Model 2 Geographic Scope:**
   Model 2 regional scoring is calibrated specifically for Maharashtra's 35 districts. Expanding to other Indian states will require importing their respective state Directorate of Economics and Statistics (DES) APY census datasets.
4. **Git Cleanliness:**
   As strictly mandated, all code modifications, tests, and documentation remain **strictly in the local working tree**. Zero commits, pushes, merges, or PR creations were executed.
